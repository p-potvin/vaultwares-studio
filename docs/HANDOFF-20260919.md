## 2. The scenery problem, root-caused

The user's long-standing complaint — the 14 June splat has background houses
that look photographic, everything since feels like a cramped room — is real,
measurable, and upstream of splatfacto.

**14 June used COLMAP. Everything since used DA3-Streaming.**

Fraction of gaussians beyond N times the core radius:

| run | SfM | beyond 2x | beyond 5x | beyond 10x |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 14 Jun | COLMAP | 12.8% | **3.6%** | **1.1%** |
| 13 Sep | DA3 | 2.2% | 0.0% | 0.0% |
| 17 Sep | DA3 | 5.5% | 0.0% | 0.0% |
| 18 Sep | DA3 | 14.9% | 0.0% | 0.0% |

Every DA3 run is exactly zero past 5x. A wall, not a falloff. The seed clouds
say why:

| seed | p95 radius | max | max/p95 |
|---|---|---|---|
| COLMAP | 6.58 | **90.34** | **13.7** |
| DA3 | 6.49 | 9.20 | 1.42 |

They **agree at p95**. DA3 is not worse in the near or mid field — on 18 Sep it
is denser, with 26x more points. What it lacks is the tail: 0.1% of points
between 20 and 90 units out. A rounding error in the count, and the entire
difference in how the scene feels.

Cause: DA3 is monocular depth truncated by `depth_threshold: 15.0` in the
Space's `_config()`, plus `conf_threshold_coef: 0.75`. Far pixels are where
monocular depth is least reliable, so they are cut. COLMAP triangulates
distant matches with no such limit. `streaming_convert.py` documented the 1.4
ratio *as a reason to skip further filtering* — the evidence was in a docstring
before anyone looked for it. Same root cause as the "neighbour's house
collapsing onto mine" artefact.

Full write-up: `docs/scenery-and-scene-bounds-20260918.md`.

## 3. Our COLMAP was running uncalibrated, and that is now fixed

Comparing the user's local database against our 14 Sep cpu-upgrade job:

| | our 14 Sep job | user's local |
|---|---|---|
| focal | 2304 px, `prior_focal=0` | 948 px, `prior_focal=1` |
| focal / width | **1.200** | 0.494 |
| config 2 CALIBRATED | **0.0%** | **85.1%** |
| config 3 UNCALIBRATED | 82.3% | 1.5% |
| median matches/pair | **36** | **754** |
| pairs verified | 46% | 83% |
| keypoints/image median | 11,486 | 4,939 |

2304 is not a measurement. It is COLMAP's default guess, 1.2 × width. Our
extractor set `single_camera` and `OPENCV` but never passed `camera_params`, so
COLMAP invented a focal 2.4x too long and marked the prior untrusted — and with
an untrusted prior it verifies pairs through the fundamental matrix instead of
the essential matrix. Hence zero calibrated pairs.

Fixed in `docker/worker/recon_entrypoint.py`: `colmap_camera_params()` builds
the eight OPENCV parameters from a calibration JSON, scaled to the resolution
COLMAP will read, refusing on aspect-ratio mismatch, leaving distortion
unscaled because it is normalised. New `--calibration`, `--sift-num-threads`,
`--match-num-threads`, `--sift-max-num-features` (default 4096). It prints an
explicit warning naming UNCALIBRATED when no calibration is supplied.
`queue_colmap_sfm.py` ships the calibration inside `worker.zip`.

**Throughput context**: our 500-image job managed 3.0 img/min; the user's
desktop projects 16.7 img/min on 2000 images. 5.6x, on ten threads against a
64-vCPU box where we were using four.

## 4. Corrections — things reported confidently and wrongly

**"The training job is about to hit a 165-minute timeout."** It was not a
timeout. Exit 137, OOMKilled, after 271 minutes. Both the mechanism and the
deadline were wrong. Cause: `cache-images cpu` holds every training image in
host RAM uncompressed — 12.4 GB of an L4's 30 GB before splatfacto allocates
anything. Arithmetic that was available before submitting. Cost $3.62 with
nothing uploaded.

**"About 1006 SALAD loop pairs."** That was the file's line count including its
1000-line image path list. Real counts: 2 (sunny500), 4 (cloudy1000), 6
(merged1600), 17 (thorough3).

**"Densification will slow the run toward the cap."** It did the reverse. The
rate climbed 5.0 → 8.3 → 10.2 → 11.3 it/s, because early steps carry dataset
load and cache warm-up. **Judge the rate at step 10,000, not step 3,000.**

**A safe flavour with an unsafe fallback.** Queued `[a10g-large, l4x1]` where
the memory gate only validated `flavors[0]`. l4x1 is the box that OOMed. Caught
while still scheduling, so unbilled. The gate now filters the whole chain.

**The drift metric was confounded.** Camera height above the ground each frame
sees mixes terrain, estimator and real drift. `tools/measure_scale_drift.py`
separates them by comparing only frames verified to be at the same physical
spot. Its own first result (median 11.86, max 281x) was also wrong — 13 of 16
pairs had a collapsed ground fit — and it now has a self-calibrating floor.

## 5. What the capture protocol bought

The 17 Sep capture list (slow walk, fixed grip height, ground in frame, door
checkpoints) produced measurably the best SfM we have:

| | sunny 500 | cloudy 1000 | merged 1600 | **thorough 2000** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| closure ratio | 0.017 | 0.092 | 0.035 | **0.001** |
| loop pairs | 2 | 4 | 6 | **17** |
| cross-clip pairs | — | — | 1 | **8** |
| seam jump vs median step | — | — | 25x | **0.08x** |

The clip seams are smaller than a footstep. Splat density is also the highest
at 1,631,279 gaussians per unit³ against 1,178,623 and 1,251,399.

**But opacity tracks the per-view iteration budget, monotonically**: 0.811 at
40 iterations/view, 0.646 at 18.75, 0.585 at 12.5. Under-converged gaussians
are translucent, and translucency accumulates along a ray — which is why the
user reports haze worst in the enclosed nook by the table and least in open
areas. Revisiting cannot fix an iteration-budget problem.

A checkpoint at step 24,999 is banked in that job's `model.zip`; `--refine-mode`
resumes rather than repeating.
