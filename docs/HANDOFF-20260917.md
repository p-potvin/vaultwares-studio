## 1. Start here: the near-field regression, and what it turned out to be

The user's report was that the 13 Sep backyard splat is blurry, that walkable
areas vanished, that a car is a smear. All true. The chain of causes, in the
order they were found:

**Training resolution never changed.** Every run trained splatfacto on
1920×1080 — verified by reading JPEG SOF headers out of each `frames.zip`, not
by trusting manifests. The low resolution is only in the SfM stage:
504×280 for DA3-Streaming against COLMAP's full 1920×1080. That is 14.7× fewer
pixels, and the frames are physically resized to disk before DA3 sees them.

**Two things the DA3 era silently dropped**, both fixed in `51dda46`:

- *Per-frame intrinsics.* DA3 estimates a focal length independently per frame:
  881.7–915.1 px in July, 875.3–913.8 px in September, from a phone whose lens
  never moved. splatfacto believed them. Now collapsed to a median and written
  once at the top level of `transforms.json`, the way COLMAP does.
- *Lens distortion.* DA3 estimates none. nerfstudio's `full_images_datamanager`
  **will** undistort, but only when `k1/k2/p1/p2` are present and non-zero — so
  on a DA3 bundle that path had never run. COLMAP's solution for this phone is
  checked in at `config/calibrations/iphone-1920x1080.json`.

Both hit the near field hardest: reprojection error scales with parallax.

## 2. Corrections to things I said during the session

Recorded because they were stated confidently and are wrong.

**"MICA is 1.84× more committed than the landmark fit."** That was coefficient
norm (5.39 vs 2.94) across a 100- and a 300-coefficient basis. FLAME's basis is
PCA-ordered, so later components barely move geometry and the comparison
rewards spreading energy into them. **In millimetres the gap nearly vanishes**:
landmark 2.17 mm mean from the FLAME mean face, MICA 2.48 mm — and MICA is
*smoother* in the tail (p95 3.66 vs 4.44). They differ from each other by
2.95 mm mean / 8.92 mm max, more than either differs from the mean face. Which
is closer to the real person is **still unmeasured**.

**"DA3's depth maps are excellent, just written under the wrong filenames."**
Stated as conclusive on the strength of individual maps correlating 0.94–0.98
with a *different* frame's pose. It does not hold. The full 80×79 correlation
matrix has 71 maps scoring ≥0.80 but only **12 distinct targets** — not a
bijection, so not a permutation. Correlation is scale- and offset-invariant, so
in a scene with a receding ground plane almost any depth map correlates highly
with any pose whose visible points span a wide depth range. The hybrid remains
unexplained.

## 4. The volume path — finished and verified

Depth maps → sparse TSDF → NanoVDB → USD → occupancy. All local, no GPU, no
OpenVDB linked.

```text
500 frames, 70.6 M depth samples, loaded 8.3s
fused 12.7s  ->  1,202,544 touched voxels  (voxel 0.0156, band +/-0.0624)
1,114,552 kept after dropping 7.3% below weight 2
wrote volume.nvdb in 0.4s — 15.9 MB, 6115 leaves / 19 lower / 8 upper
total 21.5s
```

The 43-minute COLMAP comparison was never apt: COLMAP's cost is feature matching,
quadratic-ish in image count; TSDF integration is linear in pixels. At the
production voxel size **pixel stride 2 is 4.3× faster for 8% fewer voxels**,
because the 504×280 depth maps sample finer than the 0.0156 grid.

### How it is verified, and why that matters

`nanovdb_read.py` was written **before** the writer and shares no logic with it,
because a writer validated by its own reader proves only that the two agree.
Layout transcribed from `NanoVDB.h` and cross-checked against `PNanoVDB.h`,
which states every offset as an explicit `#define`. All four `static_assert`ed
sizes match: 16 / 176 / 672 / 64.

Then the real check: **`tools/nanovdb_validate/pnvalidate.c`**, ~70 lines built
against `PNanoVDB.h` — NVIDIA's own reference reader. It walks the tree through
their read accessor and returned bit-identical values at eight probes across the
1.1 M-voxel capture, including negative coordinates. Build instructions are in
that folder's README; it needs a C compiler and nothing else. Set `INCLUDE`/`LIB`
directly rather than calling `vcvars64.bat`, which takes minutes here.

### Things in this path that fail silently, and are guarded

- **Values are world units, not `signed/sdf_trunc`.** A normalised level set
  renders fine with the isosurface displaced by the truncation factor.
- **Camera convention is OpenCV, +Z forward** — what `camera_poses.txt` holds.
  nerfstudio's `transforms.json` is OpenGL and must be rebased first. A surface
  one metre ahead is at `z = +1`; nearer the camera is *smaller* z and reads
  positive. My own first test asserted the opposite; the code was right and the
  docstring was silent.
- **The USD field relationship** is what connects volume to field. Present prim,
  missing relationship → opens cleanly, renders nothing.
- **`RootData`'s key casts signed coords to `uint32` before shifting**, so
  negatives must not sign-extend.

### Occupancy

`grid_from_level_set` *feeds* `grid_from_points` rather than replacing it, which
was the user's explicit call. On the real capture it agrees with the splat-preview
grid on extent (37×48 vs 39×47) and floor (−0.175 vs −0.154), and is better where
it differs: **13.0% free against 9.2%, 50.8% occupied against 62.7%** — the
splat's gaussians bleed into space and read as obstacles.

`scene_transform` must be `camera_scene.scene_frame_transform(job_dir)`. The
gravity rotation alone puts the floor 3 m out, because the trainer normalisation
carries a scale.

**Not used yet: the free space.** A level set distinguishes "empty" from "never
observed" and could fill the grid's UNKNOWN cells (36%). That is the obvious next
increment.

**MICA works and is the identity source.** `face_organizer/mica_identity.py`,
loads `strict=True` at 65,156,160 backbone params (matches published ArcFace
R100). Two details taken from MICA's source because the checkpoint cannot tell

followed by functional `LeakyReLU(0.2)`. `mica.safetensors` is **not** safetensors

**The ComfyUI path is finished and should not be extended.** The decisive

`Flux-Klein-9b-V2-BFS` or Qwen Image Edit 2511 are the answer if images are ever
the goal again.

## 6. Environment notes that cost time

- **The ComfyUI desktop app runs its own environment** (torch 2.10.0+cu130, CUDA,
  RTX 3060). The `.venv` inside `D:\comfyUI\resources\ComfyUI\` is stale and
  unused — CPU-only torch, no frontend package. Driving `main.py` through it
  fails in two ways unrelated to the running server. I wrongly reported the
  install as broken on that basis.
- **`miniforge3/envs/pytorch_3d`** has torch 2.2.1/cu118 and numpy 2.2.6, which
  already fails with `_ARRAY_API not found`. Building PyTorch3D there would not
  help colonel-kfc anyway (torch 2.13.0+cu126) — PyTorch3D compiles against a
  specific torch ABI. The numpy rasteriser stays.
- **HF Jobs:** the runner's 120 s flavour-scheduling timeout is too short for
  current GPU spot capacity. `--scheduling-timeout 900` is the default in the new
  launchers. A job cancelled during SCHEDULING is never billed.
- **The deployed `vw-studio-da3` image is older than the repo entrypoint.** Use
  the worker overlay (`worker.zip` over `/opt/vw`) or you will get a completed
  job missing everything added since the image was built. That cost $0.17 to
  learn.
- `hf jobs logs` dies on a Windows console: prefix `PYTHONUTF8=1 PYTHONIOENCODING=utf-8`.
